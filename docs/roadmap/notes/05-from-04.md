# Milestone 05 notes, from closing Milestone 04

**Written:** 2026-11-20, closing Milestone 04  
**Use when:** starting inventory and the basic economy

## What the world already does

A living character picks one action from scored definitions in
`packages/simulation/src/systems/actions.ts`: `idle`, `move`, `eat`,
`rest`, `drink`, `work`, `greet`, `comfort`, `keep`, `confide`, `bluff`,
`give`, `gather`, `craft`.
Commitment is five seconds. An urgent need at 0.8 interrupts and cools
the cut-off action for 20 seconds. Finishing a meal, drink, rest, or
visit does not cool it.

`work` happens on a camp while every need is quiet. The visit ends when
a need rises past 0.3. Completing it adds one `goods` to a sparse
inventory keyed by the worker's stable ID. An interruption adds nothing.
Several characters can stand on the same camp, and each keeps their own
record. A generated map also places finite piles of `material` on grass.
`gather` takes one from the pile underfoot when the visit ends. An
industrious character walks to the nearer of a camp and a pile. A tie
keeps the camp. Rock still blocks that walk. An empty pile fails and
yields nothing. Holding material at a camp crafts one food from one
material. The visit with no input still yields goods. There is still
no price, and food is not eaten yet.

Social actions are presence, not a search of the population. Greet and
comfort start beside a living neighbour. Keep, confide, and bluff start
only beside someone already met. A completed social action updates one
relationship keyed by the two stable IDs (`meetings`, `lastEventId`,
`lastTick`) and writes one log event with both participants. The first
of each kind is one shared memory pointing at that event. A bluff's
payload understates the speaker's hunger, thirst, and fatigue. It does
not change those needs, and nothing in the tick reads the payload.

Needs are still hunger, thirst, and fatigue. Personality stores ten
traits. Curiosity, industriousness, sociability, empathy, loyalty, honesty,
and greed are read. Aggression, courage, and ambition are stored stubs.

`give` can start only beside someone already met, while the actor's
needs are quiet and they hold at least one goods. It scores
`(1 - greed) * comfort`. Completing it moves one goods to that
neighbour. The log records one `item.transferred` event. The first gift
between a pair is one `giving` memory. A later gift does not add
another, and the transfer does not count as a meeting. Losing the
neighbour fails the gift. An urgent need interrupts it. Neither moves
the stack.

Saves are format version 20. A version 19 save gains a craft cooldown
of zero. A version 18 save gains resource nodes and
a gather cooldown of zero. A version 17 save gains a give cooldown of
zero. A version 16 save gains an empty inventory list. The log, memories, and relationships are
world-level lists. Inspection filters them in a straight scan.
`ACTION_IDS`, `MEMORY_KINDS`, and `SIMULATION_LOG_TYPES` are append-only.
Component arrays are process-global: a second `createSimulationWorld`
in the same test overwrites the first world's slots.

Regression for Milestone 03 and Milestone 04 is in
`packages/simulation/src/regression.test.ts`. Extend those lists. Do not
change the social expectations to make an item fit.

## What the original Milestone 05 scope assumed

The original list is still the right shape: item definitions,
inventories, ownership, resource nodes, gathering, production,
consumption, simple trade, and invariants.

Lessons from the social cycle that change how to start:

1. **A camp visit is the production hook that already exists.** Do not
   invent a second workplace. The first item should be what `work`
   yields when a visit completes, stored somewhere other than the
   action flag. The flag only says they are working.

2. **Ownership is not an all-pairs matrix.** Relationships are sparse
   records keyed by stable ID. An inventory wants the same shape: one
   record per holder, not a component slot guessed for every future
   item. A transfer is one log event with both participants, the item,
   and the quantity. Do not write "A gave" and "B received" as two facts.

3. **Greed stays unread until trade or hoarding has a rule that reads
   it.** Aggression and courage wait for conflict. Ambition waits for
   goals. A belonging need was considered in Milestone 04 and deferred;
   it is not an economy feature and it is not this milestone.

4. **Do not spend a bluff.** The payload is a claim about needs. Trade
   can later compare a claim with a transfer. Nothing in this milestone
   should treat `reportedNeeds` as stock, and nothing in the tick should
   scan the log or the memory list per character. If a trade reads
   them every tick, index by participant first (Milestone 06) rather
   than filtering the array.

5. **The save version moves when an inventory is stored.** Version 16
   has no item list. Adding one is the next version, with older saves
   gaining an empty inventory. Do not tuck item counts into an existing
   field.

6. **They still share tiles, and the route does not seek a person or a
   stockpile.** Gathering starts from the tile they are already on, the
   way work starts on a camp. A walk to a node can come after one node
   on the current tile is real.

## Suggested first increment

On a completed camp visit, add one unit of one defined item to a sparse
inventory keyed by the worker's stable ID. Inspect it. Save it. Leave
trade, prices, and greed for the increment that has two holders and a
transfer.

**Done (2026-11-20).** `goods` is the item catalog. Completing `work`
adds one to `world.inventories`, keyed by the worker's stable ID.
A later visit increments that stack. An interruption does not.
Inspection lists the stacks. Saves are version 17; a version 16 save
gains an empty list. Removing the holder drops the record. The work
log payload is unchanged: the stack is the stock, and `action.ended` is
still the fact that the visit finished. Gathering still starts from the
tile they are already on.

**Give (2026-11-21).** `give` is appended to the action list. A completed
gift moves one goods and writes one `item.transferred` event with both
participants, the item and the quantity. Greed lowers that score, so a
greedy character keeps the stack. Prices are still absent. Do not treat
`reportedNeeds` as stock.

**Gather (2026-11-21).** `material` is the second item. Generated grass
holds a pile of three. Completing `gather` while standing on one takes
a unit into that character's inventory and decrements the pile. The
pile leaves the map at zero.

**Walk (2026-11-21).** The pile route is shared and rebuilt when a pile
appears or disappears. An industrious character walks to whichever of
the camp and the pile is nearer. Equal distance keeps the camp. A lazy
character does not.

**Craft (2026-11-21).** `food` is the third item. Standing on a camp
while holding material, an industrious character crafts instead of
working. Completing the visit spends one material and adds one food.
Leaving the camp or losing the material fails the visit and spends
nothing. An interruption spends nothing. Work is still the visit with
no input. Do not eat the food yet.
